Style Guide

How to draw F10 map overlays that read like a military aviation planning chart — corridors, special-use airspace, threat laydowns and control measures — using the DCS Mission Editor drawing tools. Includes the palette, the line-style library, per-element specifications, and step-by-step manual instructions.

1 · The principle: line-work, not colored glass

A military chart conveys meaning through boundary style, line weight, and restraint — colour is a secondary cue. The DCS drawing engine is capable of this register: it is closer to a FalconView/JMPS planning overlay than a video-game minimap, if we use its full line-style set instead of flat translucent fills. Every rule in this guide maps a real cartographic convention onto a setting that exists in the Mission Editor and has been verified to save correctly.

1
Boundary style carries the airspace type. Prohibited/no-fly gets a heavy hatched border; restricted/MOA a lighter border or dash; a corridor gets a thin solid edge with a dot-dash centerline. The edge tells the reader what the area is.
2
Weight sets the hierarchy. Hard boundaries heavy (5–6), corridors medium (3), centerlines and annotations thin (1–2). Weight alone creates the read order.
3
Fill is almost invisible. Opacity 0–12%. Large areas are outlined, not flooded. A whisper of tint only for small emphasis. Saturated line over near-transparent fill.
4
One disciplined palette. Red = threat/hostile/no-fly. Cyan-blue = friendly/controlled/corridors. Magenta = special-use/advisory. Desaturated white/gold = neutral reference.
5
Type like a chart. ALL CAPS, condensed font, altitude blocks (100/GND), labels rotated to the route axis, tight black halo for legibility.
6
Symbols for points. Use the built-in tactical icons (Air Defense, Search Radar) at SAM sites — a glyph inside the ring reads as a threat instantly; a plain circle does not.
Scope & honesty. DCS has no true fill hatching (texture lives only in the line style), only circles/ovals for curves (no arcs/sectors), and text is a rotatable box, not path-following. Authenticity therefore comes from edge styling + weight + restraint + icons, never from fill.

Alpha in the ME picker is a 0–100% opacity slider. A DCS alpha byte of 0x14 ≈ 8%, 0x00 = fully transparent (outline only). For line colour keep opacity high (≈90%); for fill keep it in the 0–12% band.

5 · Worked example — Berlin Air Corridors

The full language composed on a map: three corridors converging on the control zone, over stylised Cold-War Germany terrain with a lat/long graticule. This is the register to aim for — saturated line-work, near-zero fill, chart typography.

READS AS — three published lanes into a hard control zone. Cyan = friendly/controlled; the dot-dash centerline marks each airway; fill is a whisper so the terrain and border stay legible beneath.
SETTINGS — corridor sides solid, w3; centerline dotdash, w1; CTZ solid, w3, fill 8%; labels axis-rotated with altitude block 100/GND.

5b · Worked example — strike & threat laydown

The whole palette working together: a red IADS belt, a no-fly box, targets, a fire-support line, and the friendly picture (ingress corridor, CAP, bullseye).

READS AS — at a glance: where the shooters are (red rings + AD glyphs), what to avoid (hatched keep-out), what to hit (amber), where the line is (FSCL), and your own lane/CAP/bullseye in cyan & white. No two elements share an edge style.
DISCIPLINE — every ring is solid, w2.5, fill ≤5% with an icon; the keep-out uses boundry, w2.5; friendly work stays cyan. Colour + edge + icon do all the talking — no floods.

5c · Mission chart patterns — counter-air

Ready-to-copy overlays for real mission types. Each is drawn in the same language; the labels show the control measures to place.

Defensive counter-air — CAP station

MEASURES — CAP racetracks (cyan) on the threat axis · AWACS & tanker orbits to the rear · ADIZ as a dashed advisory line · hostile WEZ rings + AD glyph north of it · bullseye for calls. Hold the line; no routed waypoints.

Offensive strike package (COMAO)

MEASURES — ingress/egress minimum-risk routes (cyan lanes + centerline) · IP marker · target ROZ (amber) with DMPIs · threat ring to skirt · SEAD position. The chart plans the geometry; the pilot flies it.

5d · Mission chart patterns — CAS & carrier

Close air support — kill box & fire-support lines

MEASURES — FLOT (strongpoint) & FSCL (dashed) frame the fight · kill box (red boundry, ticks in) opens the area beyond the FSCL · no-fire area protects the village · contact point + run-in centerline. The JTAC talks you on; the chart holds the geometry.

Carrier recovery — Case III

MEASURES — CVOA box · carrier with BRC arrow · marshal stack (dashed circle at DME) · recovery corridor + final-bearing centerline back to the ship · plane guard to starboard · recovery tanker overhead. Airspace + procedure, not a routed approach.

6 · Drawing it manually in the Mission Editor

The F10-map drawing layer is authored in the DCS Mission Editor. Overlays saved with a mission appear on every player's F10 map. Work in this order for each object.

A · Enter draw mode & pick the layer

  1. Open your mission in the Mission Editor. On the left vertical toolbar, select the Draw tool (pencil icon).
  2. In the drawing panel, set the visibility layer first: All for shared/published airspace (corridors, no-fly zones, bullseye), or Blue/Red for a coalition-private intel picture (your own threat rings). This is the single most-forgotten step.

B · Draw the geometry

  1. Choose the primitive: Line (segment / segments / freehand), Polygon (circle, oval, rectangle, free), Icon, or Text.
  2. Corridor: draw a closed free polygon as a long swath, then a separate segments line down the middle for the centerline. Zone/ring: a circle at the point, radius to scale. No-fly/restricted: a rectangle or free polygon.
  3. Place points by clicking; double-click / Enter to finish.

C · Apply the style (the authenticity step)

  1. With the object selected, open its properties panel and set, in order: Line colour (from §2, opacity ≈90%), Line thickness (from the element card), Line style (the dropdown — pick from §3), then Fill colour at the low opacity in §2 (or fully transparent).
  2. Give the object a Name that matches the label convention — it doubles as the on-map caption and keeps the layer list readable.

D · Label like a chart

  1. Add a Text object at the feature. Use ALL CAPS and the altitude-block notation (100/GND = 10,000 ft ceiling to ground; FL240/8000). Keep a small font and a dark fill for a halo.
  2. For corridors, set the text angle to the corridor's heading so the label runs along the route, not across it.
  3. At SAM sites add an Icon → Air Defense (or Search Radar) at the ring centre, scaled small.
Consistency beats effort. Draw one of each element correctly, then copy-paste and re-shape — pasted objects keep the colour, thickness, style and fill, so the whole chart stays in one visual language. Build a small "master" mission with one styled example of every element and reuse it as your palette.

7 · Basis & references

This standard is not invented — it is a DCS-drawing-tool subset of the real military and aeronautical charting authorities, implemented within the tool's limits. Each element traces to a governing document.

What it governsAuthorityBacks (in this guide)
Military symbology — unit/threat icons, boundaries, tactical graphics MIL-STD-2525D (US DoD Joint Military Symbology). Harmonized with NATO APP-6(D) / STANAG 2019 — 2525D is the free stand-in for the controlled APP-6. Air-Defense / Search-Radar glyphs at SAM sites; FLOT/FSCL symbology
Airspace control measures — corridors, no-fly, ROZ, deconfliction JP 3-52, Joint Airspace Control; ATP 3-52.1 (multiservice); NATO AJP-3.3.5 Air corridors, no-fly zones, restricted/MOA, the deconfliction line
Aeronautical charting — line styles, special-use airspace, typography FAA Aeronautical Chart Users' Guide; ICAO Annex 4 Line-style semantics, magenta special-use, altitude-block notation
As-built note (v1.13.0). The palette, weights and line styles above are the exact values in the shipped chart-style module (chartstyle.py) — one source of truth consumed by every F10 overlay. Two authenticity corrections are live: corridors are square-ended lanes (a racetrack oblong is wrong for a corridor) whose terminating end nests into the control-zone circle, and threat WEZ rings carry an Air-Defense glyph at the shooter so a ring reads as a SAM site at a glance.

Where to obtain the source documents
