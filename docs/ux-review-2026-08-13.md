# UX review — the whole app, section by section

*For discussion, 13 Aug 2026. Nothing built. Every number below is measured in
a real browser against v1.64.0, not estimated.*

---

## 1. The headline: "three clicks" is five, and the Library is the three

Rob's instinct was right, and the measurement is worse than the complaint.

**Fly Now, measured, from a cold landing page:**

| # | Click | What it costs |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Fly Now" card on the entry screen | a whole screen just to choose a door |
| 2 | Mission type (4 options) | the only genuinely interesting choice |
| 3 | Aircraft — **76 cards** | a 1,152-pixel wall |
| 4 | Map — **12 cards** | for a BFM merge, this doesn't change the fight |
| 5 | FLY | sits at y=1828 in a 1000px viewport — below the fold from the start |

**Library, measured:** entry card → mission card → *Generate & Download* =
**3 clicks and a real .miz on disk.**

So the door labelled **THREE CLICKS** takes five, and the door labelled
**CURATED** takes three. That's not a copy error to patch — it says the
information architecture and the promise were designed at different times.

### Why it's really five

The click count is a symptom. The disease is **choice volume**: 4 + 76 + 12 +
3 = **95 options on one scrolling screen** for something whose entire pitch is
"I have fifteen minutes, put me in the air."

The 76-card aircraft grid is the worst of it. A DCS pilot's identity *is*
their module set — most people fly one to three airframes and have done for
years. We ask them to re-declare it, from a 76-item list, every single time.
"My content" exists in the Library and Quick Flight only honors it if you
went and set it; nothing remembers the jet you flew last time.

---

## 2. What I'd do about it — three options

### Option A — Make the claim true (recommended)

Redesign Fly Now to genuinely be three clicks, by removing choices rather than
hiding them.

1. **Put the four mission cards on the entry screen itself.** The entry screen
   currently spends a full click asking "which door?" — but "Tanker Time /
   BFM / Kill the Guns / Beat the SAM" *is* the answer to "what do you want to
   do", and showing it costs nothing. Click 1 becomes a real choice instead of
   a navigation step.
2. **Default the jet, don't ask for it.** Remember the last aircraft flown
   (localStorage, same place `ms_recipe` already lives). Show it as a single
   chip — "Flying: **F-16C**  ·  change" — with the full grid one click behind
   the "change". First-ever visit falls back to the most-flown module in the
   analytics data. Click 2 is the jet only if you want a different one.
3. **Default the map, don't ask for it.** This is the sharp one: **for
   practice reps, the map is not a meaningful choice.** A merge over Caucasus
   and a merge over Syria are the same fight — the map only changes the
   scenery and whether you own it. Pick a free, era-legal, owned map by
   default, show it as a chip with a reroll, and put the full list behind
   "change". Click 3 is **FLY**.

Result: **3 clicks for the common case**, every option still reachable, and
the screen fits without scrolling. The dice (reroll) becomes more valuable,
not less: it's how you get variety once you're not manually re-choosing.

**Cost:** moderate. Entry screen restructure, a "remembered defaults" layer,
and Fly Now becomes a compact one-screen form.

### Option B — Change the promise

Relabel to "Practice in a minute" and drop the click count. Cheap, honest, and
gives up the actual win.

### Option C — Keep both, fix the label

Make the Library's card say "Three clicks" (it's true) and Fly Now say
something else. Cheapest, and it correctly identifies which path is fastest —
but leaves the 76-card wall in place.

**My recommendation: A.** B and C fix the sentence; A fixes the product. The
work in A is also the work that makes every other path faster, because
"remember what this pilot flies" is reusable everywhere.

## 3. Section-by-section

### Entry screen
- **Costs a click on every single path** and carries no information a pilot
  can act on. Its three cards are *navigation*, not *choices*.
- The "TWO FLAVORS OF MISSION" explainer below the fold is genuinely useful
  and almost nobody scrolls to it.
- **Proposal:** entry becomes the Fly Now screen — mission cards live here —
  with Library and Builder as persistent top-bar destinations (they already
  are). First-run visitors get the explainer inline; returning visitors get
  their last jet and a FLY button.

### Fly Now
- 95 options, 1,891px tall, FLY below the fold. Covered above.
- The spice selector (Calm / Realistic / Hostile) is well-designed and *is* a
  real choice — keep it visible, it's cheap.
- The hint line ("Modern · seed 42445 — era, base, weather and comms are set
  for you") is excellent: it tells the pilot what we decided on their behalf.
  **That pattern should be the model for the whole app**: decide, then say
  what you decided.

### Library — the strongest section
- 42 cards, 3 clicks to a mission, filters that work. This is the product's
  best path and it's labelled as the slow one.
- **But**: 8 role tabs + 6 filter dropdowns for 42 items is a heavier
  apparatus than the content needs. Only 8 cards are above the fold.
- **Proposal:** collapse the six dropdowns into one "Filters" affordance that
  opens when wanted; keep the role tabs (they're one click and visual). Sort
  by "what you can fly right now" when ownership is known — a locked card is
  a card that wastes a slot in the grid.

### Builder — 13 sections, 19 selects, 121 inputs, 64 cards
- This is the "full control" door and complexity is the *point*; the rail and
  the six-screen flow handle it well.
- The real risk is **first-run intimidation**, not the option count. A pilot
  arriving from "Open in Builder to tweak" is oriented; one arriving cold from
  the entry screen faces 121 inputs.
- **Proposal:** no restructure. Add a one-line "what you'll get if you change
  nothing" summary at the top — the Builder already has sensible defaults for
  every field, and it never says so. Same "decide, then say what you decided"
  pattern as the Fly Now hint.

### Post-generate Mission Kit
- Correct instinct, correctly placed. The one gap: it appears and the page
  doesn't move to it, so on a long Builder screen it can render below the
  fold. **Scroll it into view and announce it** (the live region exists now).

### Cross-cutting: nothing is remembered
- `ms_recipe` remembers the Builder's state; `ms_owned` remembers modules if
  you declared them; **nothing remembers what you actually flew.**
- One small store — last aircraft, last map, last mission type — would make
  Fly Now three clicks, pre-sort the Library, and pre-fill the Builder. It's
  the single highest-leverage change in this document.

## 4. Suggested phasing

| Phase | Content | Why this order |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | "Remembered pilot" store (last jet / map / type) + Fly Now defaults-with-override + FLY above the fold | Makes the three-click promise true; unblocks everything else |
| **2** | Entry screen becomes Fly Now; Library/Builder promoted to top-bar-only navigation | Removes a click from every path |
| **3** | Library filter consolidation + own-first sorting; Builder "if you change nothing" summary; Kit scroll-into-view | Polish on already-good sections |

Phases 1 and 2 are one release together if you want the entry restructure in
the same breath as the defaults; splitting them lets you see the click count
drop before committing to the bigger IA change.

---

## 5. Questions for you

1. **Entry screen**: do you want it gone (Option A, Fly Now becomes the
   landing) or kept as a chooser? It's the biggest IA decision here.
2. **Map-as-default for practice reps** — do you agree the map is scenery for
   a BFM/tanker rep, or is picking the map part of the fun?
3. **Remembered jet**: silent (just works) or visible ("welcome back — still
   flying the Hornet?")? Silent is faster; visible is friendlier and teaches
   people the app remembers.
