# How this pipeline works

*Learn it, fly it, fight it. Five minutes; read it once.*

## Three schools, in order

Every air force trains a pilot the same way, and this pipeline copies it because it works.

**School 1 — Ground School and UPT** teaches you to fly an airplane. Ground school opens with the history chapter, because an aircraft's habits come from what it was built to be; after that nothing in the school is about your jet in particular: holding a formation position, flying a card on the clock, taking fuel from a tanker. Fly it in the jet the pipeline is built for if you own it; fly it in whatever you own if you do not. The lessons transfer.

**School 2 — FRS (Know Your Jet)** teaches you *this* airplane: the contact phase (start, taxi, pattern), systems, the squadron's own tactical checkout, the weapons deliveries, and a check ride flown with no coaching.

**School 3 — MQT (Fight the Jet)** is mission qualification: the airplane goes to war. Routed strikes, timed packages, the historic corridors.

## What a unit is

A unit is one of four things. A **reading** is a chapter like this one. A **track** is an ordered set of generated rides with its own printed guide and a one-click download of the whole thing. A **card** is a single Library mission. A **planned** unit is one that is not built yet — it is shown with the reason, because a syllabus that hides its gaps looks finished and is not.

Every ride is generated by Sortie Starter, so it is deterministic: the same ride is the same mission for you and for the person checking you. Each ships with a printed brief and an in-jet kneeboard. Where the Mission Editor can see what you are doing — altitude, speed, position, timing — the ride grades you inside the mission and opens a scorecard. Where it cannot (switchology, a good lock, a plug on the boom), the card says so plainly rather than pretending.

## Your record, and the squadron's

Tick a unit done when you have flown it to the standard on its brief. That record lives in your browser and nowhere else; nothing about your progress is sent anywhere. A squadron that wants to run the pipeline takes the **squadron kit**: the printed program, a gradesheet with one row per ride for an instructor to sign, and the readings. The missions come from each track's own download, so the kit stays small and the squadron always gets the current build.

## The standard

The brief on each ride is the standard. "Complete" means you met it — not that you landed. If a squadron's own standards differ, theirs govern; the kit's gradesheet leaves room for that.
